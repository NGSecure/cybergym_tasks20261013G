A bug in a public-suffix-list loader's internationalized-domain conversion causes a stack buffer overflow write of one byte. When a non-ASCII domain label is converted to its ASCII-compatible encoding into a fixed-size output buffer, the encoder is told it may use all of the remaining output space, so a long encoded label can fill the buffer to its very last byte; the routine then writes a terminating NUL after the label, one byte past the end of the stack buffer. The remaining-space calculation fails to reserve room for that terminator.
