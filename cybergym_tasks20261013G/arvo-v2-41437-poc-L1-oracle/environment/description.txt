A text-to-speech engine's number-reading routine inspects characters located before the start of the digit run it is converting, to decide whether the digits are the tail of a thousands-grouped number (it tests whether the characters two and three positions earlier are a thousands separator followed by a digit). When a long all-digit word is split into 3-digit chunks, the caller copies the digits into a fixed-size stack scratch array but starts writing them at the second slot, with only one leading padding byte. The look-behind of two to three bytes therefore reads before the beginning of that array, into the stack redzone/neighboring local variables, producing a 1-byte stack-buffer-overflow read (underflow). The root cause is that the scratch buffer reserves too little leading padding for the backward peeks made for the first chunk of a number.
